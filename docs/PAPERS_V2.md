# Paper-grounded higher-risk research: v2

Specification written 1 November 2026, before the v2 aggregate screen. The user's new instruction authorizes higher-risk research and a 10% monthly target. It does not establish an achievable return or approve a funded portfolio. Source access ranges from full working papers to publisher/author abstracts; the table identifies the distinction. No paper's published return is presented as our reproduced result.

## The research question

Can a fixed, implementable rule improve net compounded returns relative to an appropriate passive exposure, after costs and without using future observations? A second, much harder question is whether its geometric monthly growth reaches 10%. That means 213.84% compounded annually. An arithmetic average of 10% is not equivalent: +50%, then −30% averages +10% but compounds to only +2.47% per month. Occasional 10% months also do not establish that growth rate.

Our hypothesis set contains twelve new candidates from nine paper sources. Signals are deterministic. An LLM does not choose positions or tune rules during a run. Common lookbacks and fixed sizing constants are declared in the configuration, not optimized after seeing the table. Papers supply testable mechanisms, not a guarantee that an ETF adaptation inherits their findings.

The twelfth candidate, `industry_mom_volscaled`, combines the six-month top-three sector rule with a 63-session covariance estimate. Scale its total 95% allocation downward when estimated annualized basket volatility exceeds 30%. This is a **hybrid inverse-volatility adaptation**, not the inverse-variance rule or a separate paper replication. Equal sector weights and long-only constraints can leave its behavior close to the unscaled rule. Count it as a trial, not independent evidence.

## Higher-risk implementation and appropriate benchmarks

The original v1 research allocation was 25%. V2 candidates can invest 95%, with some holding daily 3× ETFs: roughly 285% underlying daily exposure initially. The remaining cash does not insure the investment. Holdings drift between scheduled rebalances; 95% is not a continuous cap. Account borrowing and shorting are absent, but leverage exists inside the funds.

[ProShares TQQQ documentation](https://www.proshares.com/our-etfs/leveraged-and-inverse/tqqq) specifies a daily objective. Multi-day compounded returns need not equal three times the index's return. We use actual TQQQ/UPRO/QLD histories, including observed financing/expense effects, instead of multiplying monthly returns by three. TQQQ starts in 2010; its observed history does not include the dot-com crash or 2008.

Seven references: zero-interest cash; 95%-initial buy-and-hold SPY, QQQ, QLD, TQQQ, UPRO and BIL. Distributions accumulate in cash. TQQQ timing is compared primarily with TQQQ holding, UPRO timing with UPRO holding, sector selection with SPY. The relative leveraged-switch rule is compared with TQQQ; that does not fully isolate dynamic Nasdaq-versus-S&P beta. These are return comparisons, not factor-regression alpha estimates.

## Latency and research we cannot honestly reproduce yet

We have not measured the user's broker round trip or feed delay. Daily/weekly/monthly rules are plausible for a local Singapore workstation because their signals do not depend on winning a microsecond race. Overnight announcements, opening auctions and broker outages still affect fills. The one- and three-session delays test stale information; they are not millisecond latency measurements. HFT market making, queue arbitrage and cross-venue arbitrage require a different infrastructure and dataset.

Two intraday papers deserve a separate next experiment, using time-stamped minute bars and realistic execution:

1. Gao, Han, Li & Zhou, [Market Intraday Momentum](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2552752): early-session information predicting late-session returns. Investigate explicit session boundaries, overnight-return inclusion, volatility/volume conditions, and next-bar execution. Publisher/author abstract reviewed; not reproduced here.
2. Zarattini, Aziz & Barbon, [Beat the Market: An Effective Intraday Momentum Strategy for SPY](https://concretumgroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Beat-the-Market.pdf), revised September 2025: noise-region breakouts, volatility sizing and trailing stops/VWAP. The authors report 19.6% annualized net return and Sharpe 1.33 for 2007–early 2024. These are their backtest figures, not ours, and are far below 10% monthly compounding. Reproduction requires historical intraday price/volume, careful stop/entry ordering and paper-matched costs. Daily bars cannot establish those results.

We have not purchased data or connected a broker. Do not substitute random prices for real historical evidence, or claim an unrun intraday method is one of the completed simulations. A $2,000 cash account's settled funds and per-order costs also constrain repeated intraday round trips. Broker eligibility and any leveraged-product permissions require separate confirmation.

## Data, execution and corporate-action amendments

The inherited engine uses next-open fills, prior NAV, assumed costs, three-session sale-proceeds settlement and 30-session distribution cash delay. See [v1 methodology](METHODOLOGY.md) for mechanics; v2 overrides allocation, universe, explicit cadence and taxable symbols. Base costs: 5 bp one way, $0.40 per order; 30% withholding on all non-BIL distributions. These are research approximations, not broker quotes. $10/month overhead, higher commissions, higher slippage, delayed signals, whole shares, half capital, all-distribution tax and half exposure are separate sensitivities. FX and exact tax treatment remain unresolved.

Some scenario outputs deliberately coincide: the tax stress only changes BIL because all candidate-fund distributions already receive 30% withholding; stale-signal scenarios do not change a constant buy-and-hold instruction; and passive benchmark allocations stay fixed when candidate allocation is halved. Do not count duplicate outcomes as additional evidence.

Known post-window splits are restored from [ProShares' 2025 notice](https://www.proshares.com/press-releases/proshares-announces-etf-share-splits5) and [the sector funds' SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1064641/000119312526256847/d128483dncsrs.htm). These are corporate-action facts, not post-2024 price bars. A complete independent action audit remains outstanding.

XLF's 2016 vendor ratio 1.231 represents an XLRE spin-off, not additional parent shares. The [SEC notice](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1064641/000119312516719418/R3.htm) establishes the distribution; [S&P's methodology](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/jp/documents/methodologies/methodology-enhanced-cov-call-strat-indices.pdf) records $4.443564 per XLF share. V2 restores original units, removes the fictitious share multiplication, and credits that amount as a delayed cash-equivalent distribution with conservative 30% tax. This approximates disposing of XLRE, without its actual price path, sale costs or payable date. Sector results are provisional, not exact corporate-action replication. Price-high signals retain vendor corporate-action-adjusted price units.

## Validation and evidence standard

2016–2024 was already inspected in v1 and is reused here. 2011–2015 is separately reported, with all instruments available. Both are retrospective; 2025+ price bars remain unfetched, but general market knowledge and published research already inform design. Ten cost/risk scenarios overlap, so 120 candidate runs do not mean 120 independent datasets.

Report all candidates, compounded and arithmetic monthly returns, every losing month, worst month, drawdown, 10%-month frequency, earlier-window results, scenario deterioration and benchmark comparisons. A paired three-month circular block bootstrap supplies exploratory intervals and one-sided tests. Two hypothesis families across 22 declared v1/v2 candidates imply Bonferroni factor 44. This is not a proof against overfitting, a full search-history correction, or a substitute for fresh evidence. [Bailey et al.'s backtest-overfitting paper](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-prob.pdf) motivates retaining all trials.

[Daniel & Moskowitz's Momentum Crashes](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20439) provides a further warning: momentum can have severe state-dependent reversals. Its long-short mechanism is not identical to these long-only ETFs, but a benign average alone is insufficient. No stop rule can guarantee a maximum loss through an opening gap.

Promotion requires a frozen hypothesis that survives a fresh evaluation, an independently checked data/cost model, and prospective paper orders recorded before outcomes. New variants require a new registry entry. Finding a higher-return backtest after repeated searching is not enough to fund it.
